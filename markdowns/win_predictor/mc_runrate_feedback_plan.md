# Monte Carlo Run-Rate Feedback — Eliminating the `current_team_score` Loop

> **Author:** Antigravity AI · **Date:** 2026-08-09
> **Purpose / Strategy:** Break the positive feedback loop in which the simulator's own accumulated score is fed back to the delivery-outcome model as a feature, amplifying any run-rate error until it settles ~14% high
> **Baseline / Metrics:** od MC calibration — innings bias **+18.17 ± 4.88** (gate ≤13.0, FAILING), MAE **46.69** (gate ≤40.6, FAILING), win-prob agreement 0.0958 ✅, O/U Brier 0.1982 ✅, coverage 0.92 ✅. t20 — bias **−1.16** ✅, MAE **27.57** (gate ≤25.0, FAILING)
> **Defaults / Scope:** Simulator only. The delivery-outcome model is **measured correct** and is not the target; the win-probability and first-innings models are untouched and out of scope entirely.

---

## Executive Summary

`current_team_score` is simultaneously a **model input** and a **simulation output**. In training that is
legitimate — a high score at a given point genuinely predicts continued fast scoring (good pitch,
strong batting side), and the model learned that correlation correctly. Inside a simulation it is
circular: the score is generated by the model's own assumptions, then fed back as evidence about
conditions it invented.

Measured behaviour of the loop (od, 2026-08-09):

| Evidence | Value |
|---|---|
| Model calibration on real states (2.1M deliveries) | runs **+0.01%**, P(W) slightly *high* in every over bucket |
| Simulator run rate vs actual | 5.41 vs 5.00 rpo (**+0.41**, ~3 SE) |
| Only state feature that drifts (200 matches, match-level paired) | `current_team_score` **+10.68, t = +5.01** |
| All other tracked features (wickets_lost, batter/bowler Elo, striker_balls_faced, matchup_elo_diff) | **t between −1.06 and +0.96 — none significant** |
| Model sensitivity to the drift | +10.68 score ⇒ **+0.236 rpo** |
| Share of the run-rate excess explained | **58%** (⚠️ superseded — see below) |

Arithmetic closes: +0.41 rpo × 45.5 overs ≈ +18.7 runs vs the measured +18.17 bias. The loop has
gain < 1, so it equilibrates at a stable ~14% score inflation rather than diverging — which is why
the by-over profile plateaus instead of exploding.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **What this plan will and will not fix.** Removing the explained 58% should cut od's bias from
> **+18.17 to ≈ +7.6**, clearing the ≤13.0 gate. It will **not** fix MAE. MAE is dominated by
> irreducible spread — removing the entire offset moves it only 0.7% — and the 40.6 target needs
> ~47% of innings-total variance explained pre-match versus ~30% achieved today. Treat the MAE gate
> as a separate question (see `Deferred` below); do not judge this plan by it.

> [!WARNING]
> **Update (Phase 3, 2026-08-10): the 58%/+7.6 figures above were arithmetic, and turned out to
> overstate the loop's real effect.** Two independent DIRECT simulations at full damping (Phase
> 1's sweep and Phase 3's own re-measurement, different match samples) both found the loop
> explains only **~35–36%** of the baseline bias — at full damping od's bias lands at **+10.04
> to +11.33**, not +7.6. The ≤13.0 gate still clears (Phase 1 shipped shrink=0.30, bias +12.72 at
> full canonical fidelity), so nothing here changes what shipped — but the "explained 58%" framing
> throughout this doc predates that correction. See Phase 3's Results section for the full
> decomposition and why the arithmetic was optimistic (a single blended sensitivity constant
> doesn't capture the by-over-bucket tapering Phase 0 itself measured).

## The t20 Tension — read before starting

od's bias is **+18.17** (too high). t20's is **−1.16** (very slightly low, comfortably passing).
Any fix that reduces simulated scoring moves **both** formats down. Scaled by innings length, a
change worth ~10.5 runs in od is worth ~4 runs in t20, which would push t20's bias to roughly
**−5** — still inside ±8, but materially worse than today.

> [!CAUTION]
> This is a genuine trade: the change is *correct* in both formats (the loop is an artefact
> everywhere), but it only *helps the gate* in od. Every phase therefore carries an independent
> per-format keep/revert decision, and t20's bias must be re-measured, not assumed. If t20's bias
> breaches ±8, prefer keeping the fix and re-examining t20's level separately over reinstating a
> known artefact to mask it.

## Phase 0: Characterise the Loop

**Files:** `scripts/measure_score_feedback.py` (NEW) · **Priority:** 🔴 Critical · **Retrain:** No

> [!NOTE]
> **Completed 2026-08-09.** Full results in `outputs/score_feedback_{od,t20}.json` and the par
> trajectories in `outputs/score_trajectory_{od,t20}.json` (also written for `hnd` from an
> earlier smoke-test run). Summary below; see the `Implementation Tracking` table at the foot of
> this doc for the one-line version.

### Problem

Three unknowns gate the design:

1. **Does t20 have the same loop, and how strong?** All measurements so far are od-only. t20's
   sensitivity (rpo per run of score) must be measured on its own corpus before any shared fix.
2. **How much real signal would be lost?** `current_team_score` is presumably a genuinely useful
   feature. Phase 2 is only viable if dropping it costs little log-loss.
3. **Is the steep response plausible or overfit?** Verified 2026-08-09: `delivery_outcome_model.py`
   applies **no monotone constraints** (unlike `xgb_win_probability.py`, a different model — do not
   confuse the two). So the ~0.022 rpo-per-run slope is entirely learned, and its *shape* is worth
   inspecting: a smooth economically-sensible curve is a real signal to preserve, whereas a steep
   or non-monotonic one is a candidate for regularisation rather than removal.

### Implementation

1. **Sensitivity curve per format.** Repeat the +5/+10.68/+20 perturbation sweep for t20 (and
   optionally hnd), reporting rpo per run of score. Record whether the response is linear.
2. **Partial-dependence shape.** Plot E[runs] against `current_team_score` across its real range
   (not just the three perturbation points) to distinguish genuine signal from an overfit slope.
3. **Ablation cost.** Retrain the legal-outcome head **out-of-fold only** with
   `current_team_score` (a) dropped and (b) replaced by `score_minus_par_trajectory`, and report
   OOF log-loss vs the locked `sim_baseline_metrics.json`. This is the retrain-cost estimate; it
   does not ship anything.
4. **Feature importance** for context.


od's slope at +10.68 (+0.02242) reproduces the plan's headline number almost exactly (+0.0236
quoted in the Executive Summary), and the arithmetic closes cleanly: `+0.02242 × 45.5 overs ×

sampling noise, using an independent measurement (out-of-fold perturbation vs. the earlier

t20's pooled slope (+0.0332) is **~50% steeper than od's per run of score** — the earlier
plan text implicitly assumed od's mechanism was the stronger one; per-run it's the opposite.
t20's bias currently passes (−1.16) only because its measured *score drift* is small, not
because its model is less reactive to score — worth remembering if t20's drift ever grows

**2. Shape — response is genuinely monotone increasing in od/t20, at every over bucket.**
Both formats show the slope *tapering* through the innings (od: +0.040 rpo/run in overs 0-12 →

the partial-dependence curves are monotone in all 8 over-buckets checked (4 per format). No
monotone constraint exists on either head today. **Conclusion: this is real, economically
sensible signal (a higher score at a given point really does predict a stronger batting side),
not an overfit spike** — which rules out Phase 2's "regularise the slope" option and argues
against outright dropping the feature.

> The hnd smoke-test run (skipped from the full pass; n=20k legal deliveries, not the full

**3. Ablation cost — both variants affordable; `replace` is effectively free.**

| Format | drop Δ log-loss | drop t (n=320 matches) | replace Δ log-loss | replace t |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| od | +0.002625 | +6.56 (real, but small) | **−0.000083** | −0.75 (noise) |
| t20 | +0.001451 | +2.79 (real, but small) | +0.000334 | 0.24 (noise) |

Both subsampled at 800/n_matches matches, 2/5 GroupKFold folds, control-relative (never against
the full-corpus lock — od's own subsampled control already sits +0.0031 above the lock, t20's
+0.0126 above, for reasons unrelated to the feature). `drop` costs a real but small amount of
log-loss in both formats (comfortably inside the sim_baseline_metrics.json +0.01 gate tolerance

(`score_minus_par_trajectory`) is statistically indistinguishable from the control in both
formats** — centring the feature on the par trajectory removes the circularity essentially for
free. This makes Phase 2 option (1) (replace) the cheap, principled fix if Phase 1's damping
alone falls short — no need to fall back to (2) regularise or (3) drop.

**4. Audit — no monotone constraints anywhere; `current_team_score` is a top-3 feature by gain
in both heads** (od: rank 2/15, 10.8% gain share; t20: rank 2/15, 4.5% gain share — od's legal

**Par score trajectory** (needed by Phase 1's damping and Phase 2's `replace` variant) was
measured and written to `outputs/score_trajectory_{od,t20,hnd}.json`, both as the plan's

variant (every innings that reached that ball, i.e. conditioned on "still batting" — the state
the simulator is actually in). Innings 2's `full_length` curve is a biased-low sample by
construction (a chase that used all its balls is one that didn't win comfortably); Phase 1
should pick the definition deliberately rather than default to `full_length` for both innings.

## Phase 1: Simulation-Side Damping (no retrain)

**Files:** `src/simulation/innings_simulator.py` (MODIFY) · **Priority:** 🔴 High · **Retrain:** No

### Problem

The loop can be broken without touching the model at all: feed the model a **shrunk** score rather
than the raw simulated one. In training, `current_team_score` is the *real* score — an unbiased
observation of the latent match state. In simulation it is a self-generated estimate carrying the
model's own error. Shrinking it toward the expected trajectory reduces the amplification while
keeping most of the genuine signal.

### Implementation

1. **Measure the par score trajectory per format** — mean cumulative score at each ball index over
   full-length innings only, mirroring `BOWLER_OVER_SHARES`'s methodology (full innings only, so
   rain-shortened and all-out innings don't distort the curve). Store as a per-format vector.
2. **Shrink at the point of use** in `_assemble_state_matrix`:
   ```python
   expected = SCORE_TRAJECTORY[format_type][ball_index]
   fed_score = expected + SCORE_FEEDBACK_SHRINK * (score[idx] - expected)
   ```
   `SCORE_FEEDBACK_SHRINK = 1.0` reproduces today's behaviour exactly (byte-identical), which is
   the safety property that makes this reversible.
3. **Sweep** `shrink ∈ {0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}` and record bias/MAE/coverage per format.
4. Pick the smallest intervention that clears od's bias gate — not necessarily the lowest bias.

### Verification

```bash
py tests/one-time/generate_mc_calibration_metrics.py --format od --n-sims 1000   # writes a _fast_ file
py tests/one-time/generate_mc_calibration_metrics.py --format t20 --n-sims 1000
# final candidate only, at full fidelity:
py tests/one-time/generate_mc_calibration_metrics.py --format od
```

Expected at the chosen shrink: od bias ≈ **+7–8** (from +18.17), MAE roughly unchanged (~46.5),
coverage still inside [0.70, 0.95], t20 bias no worse than **−5**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Train/serve mismatch is the real risk here.** With `shrink < 1` the model receives score values
> whose dispersion is narrower than anything in training — it is being asked to extrapolate off its
> training distribution. Watch coverage and `avg_std_dev` as the canaries: if the simulated
> distribution collapses (coverage climbing toward 0.95+, std-dev falling out of range) the damping
> is suppressing legitimate variance, not just feedback. That failure mode argues for Phase 2 over
> a more aggressive shrink.

### Results (2026-08-10)

**Implementation deviated from the plan's literal spec in two ways, both deliberate:**

1. **Trajectory definition.** Uses the **all_reaching** curve (mean score at ball B over every
   innings that reached ball B), not the plan's originally-specified `full_length`
   (full-quota/all-out only). The simulator is always in a live, still-batting state when it
   looks the trajectory up — exactly what `all_reaching` measures — and `full_length`'s innings-2
   curve is measurably biased low (a chase that used its whole quota is one that didn't chase
   comfortably; see Phase 0's Results). Built by `scripts/build_score_trajectory.py` from Phase
   0's `outputs/score_trajectory_{fmt}.json`, written to the canonical
   `data/processed/{fmt}_score_trajectory.json` the simulator loads at runtime.
2. **Per-format shrink, not one shared constant.** `SCORE_FEEDBACK_SHRINK` is a `{format: value}`
   dict (mirroring `BOWLER_OVER_SHARES`'s existing pattern), not the single scalar the plan's
   pseudocode showed — Phase 0 measured materially different loop gain per run of score
   (od +0.0224 rpo/run, t20 +0.0326), so there was no reason to expect one value fits both.

**Much finer shrink resolution than the plan's 5-point grid**, per this session's explicit
request: `tests/score_feedback_shrink_sweep.py` sweeps 21 points (step 0.05) instead of
`{0, .25, .5, .75, 1}`, packing all 21 candidates into ONE `simulate_innings()` call per match
(a new array form of `score_feedback_shrink`, `simulate_innings(..., score_feedback_shrink=
<length-n_sims array>)` — lets a caller give every simulated row its own shrink value, so the
~total_balls delivery-loop overhead is paid once per match covering the whole grid, not once per
candidate). 100 matches × 70 sims/point per format, ~60 min (od) / ~25 min (t20).

| Format | Raw bias range (shrink 1→0) | OLS fit | R² | Fitted crossing (bias=gate) | Chosen shrink |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| od | +17.35 → +11.33 (noisy, ±0.7 run point-to-point) | bias = 11.20 + 5.11·shrink | 0.83 | ~0.35 (Theil-Sen: ~0.33) | **0.30** |
| t20 | −1.65 → −0.78 (never near the ±8.0 gate) | bias = −0.85 − 0.56·shrink | 0.38 | n/a — never crosses | **1.0 (no damping)** |

The sweep's own naive "smallest shrink that clears the gate" logic (linear interpolation
between the two adjacent RAW grid points nearest the crossing) picked od's shrink=0.48 — thrown
off by a local noise dip. A **linear regression across all 21 points** (R²=0.83, confirming
Phase 0's near-linear prediction) is far more robust and put the true crossing at ~0.33–0.35;
**0.30 was chosen with margin below that** rather than exactly on it, since the fitted crossing
itself carries ~±0.14 uncertainty (residual SD ÷ slope) and the expensive full-fidelity
confirmation run should not be a coin flip.

**t20 needs no damping at all** — its bias barely moves across the *entire* [0,1] range (a
~0.9-run swing) despite Phase 0 finding t20's per-run-of-score sensitivity is actually *steeper*
than od's (+0.0326 vs +0.0224 rpo/run). The loop's effect on bias is sensitivity × drift, and
t20's simulator apparently doesn't drift far from the real trajectory to begin with. This
resolves the plan's "t20 tension" section cleanly: applying od's 0.30 to t20 would have moved
its bias from −1.16 to only ≈ −1.2 to −1.5 (interpolating the sweep table), nowhere near the
±8.0 gate — the feared trade-off never materialises.

**Fast-fidelity confirmation** (`generate_mc_calibration_metrics.py --n-sims 1000`, the plan's

## Phase 2: Model-Side De-Circularisation (only if Phase 1 falls short)

**Files:** `src/models/delivery_outcome_model.py` (MODIFY) · **Priority:** 🟡 Medium · **Retrain:** Yes

### Problem

Damping treats the symptom while leaving a feature the model will always over-trust in simulation.
The principled fix is to stop giving the model a circular conditions-proxy and instead let it read
conditions from information the simulation **cannot manufacture**: `venue_avg_first_innings_score`,
team/player Elos, phase.

### Implementation

Pick per Phase 0's ablation cost, cheapest first:

1. **Replace** `current_team_score` with `score_minus_par_trajectory` (deviation from expected).
   Retains the "ahead/behind par" signal but centres it, so simulation drift enters as a small
   deviation rather than a large level — and a centred feature has far less room to compound.
2. **Regularise** the learned response if Phase 0's partial-dependence plot shows the slope is
   steeper than the data supports (e.g. a monotone constraint or depth/leaf-weight limit on this
   feature — note none exist today, so this would be *adding* one deliberately).
3. **Drop** the feature outright. Only if (1) and (2) fail and Phase 0 showed the log-loss cost is
   acceptable.

Mechanical uses of the score must be preserved regardless — `required_run_rate` in innings 2 is
computed from the score and is structurally necessary, not a conditions proxy.

### Verification

```bash
py tests/sim_baseline_metrics.py --formats od t20     # the retrain gate
py tests/one-time/generate_mc_calibration_metrics.py --format od
py tests/one-time/generate_mc_calibration_metrics.py --format t20
```

Gate: OOF log-loss within the locked tolerance (`extras`/`legal` +0.01), **and** od bias under 13.0.
A variant that fixes bias but breaks log-loss is not acceptable — that trades a simulation artefact
for a genuinely worse per-ball model.

## Phase 3: Re-measure the Unexplained Residual

**Files:** reuse `scratchpad/state_drift_paired.py` · **Priority:** 🟡 Medium · **Retrain:** No

### Problem

Only **58%** of the run-rate excess is attributed. The remaining ~0.17 rpo (~7.6 runs) is invisible
in every feature mean tested — all five non-score features came back non-significant at n=200. It
therefore lives in the **joint distribution or sequencing**, not in any marginal.

### Implementation

1. Re-run the match-level paired drift test after Phase 1/2 and confirm `current_team_score` drift
   has collapsed toward zero (the direct check that the intervention did what it claims).
2. If residual bias persists, compare **distributions** rather than means — e.g. the joint
   distribution of (`wickets_lost`, `over_float`), and the partnership/dismissal *sequence*, which
   no marginal mean can reveal.

> [!NOTE]
> Keep the match as the unit of independence. Pooling deliveries during this investigation turned a
> 1-SE difference into an apparent 5.8-SE finding and produced a confident, entirely false
> conclusion that the simulator under-takes early wickets. At n=200 matches, `|t| > 2` is the bar.

### Results (2026-08-10)

**Files:** `scripts/measure_residual_drift.py` (NEW — this session's temp scratchpad from Phase
0 no longer exists; rebuilt from its captured methodology, extended to pack THREE shrink
candidates — no-damping baseline (1.0), production, full-damping (0.0) — into one
`simulate_innings()` call per match, so the decomposition below and Phase 1's fed-score check
share a single simulation run instead of three separate passes).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Correction to the Executive Summary's "58% explained" figure.** That number came from
> arithmetic (measured score drift × measured sensitivity), which assumed the effect scales
> linearly all the way down to zero drift. Phase 1's shrink sweep and this Phase 3 re-measurement
> are **two independent direct simulations** at shrink=0 (full damping) — not arithmetic — and
> both land at the same place: **the loop explains ~35–36% of od's baseline bias, not 58%.**
> Roughly **64% of the original bias is genuinely unexplained by this loop** and was never
> reachable by damping, however aggressively. The arithmetic's single blended sensitivity
> constant, applied uniformly, doesn't capture the tapering Phase 0's own by-over-bucket table
> already showed (od: +0.040 rpo/run early → +0.017 late) — worth remembering before trusting a
> similar linear extrapolation elsewhere in this codebase.

**Q1 — did Phase 1 do what it claims?** Re-running the paired drift test against the LIVE damped
simulator (n=200 matches each):

| Format | `current_team_score` drift (pre-Phase-1) | `current_team_score` drift (post-Phase-1, at production shrink) | Other 5 features |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| od | +10.68 (t=+5.01) | **+5.01 (t=+2.31)** | all `\|t\|` < 1.4, non-significant |
| t20 | not separately re-measured pre-Phase-1 (shrink=1.0 unchanged) | +0.63 (t=+0.62) | 5/6 non-significant; `bowler_elo` t=+2.67 (small, +2.8 Elo pts / ~0.2% — plausible false positive at 6 comparisons, not investigated further) |

od's fed-score drift roughly halved (damping mechanism confirmed working), though it did **not**
collapse to zero — expected, since production shrink=0.30 only partially damps by design (Phase

## MAE Gate Follow-Up (2026-08-11) — scaling fixed, skill ceiling quantified

Triggered by the question "is MAE nearly fully optimised, or can we get under the gate?". Answer:
**for od it is essentially optimised and the gate was mis-derived; for t20 there is genuine
modelling headroom but a hard target.** Two separable findings, one of which was actionable
immediately and has now shipped.

### Finding 1 (SHIPPED) — the cross-format gate scaling was provably wrong

`_calibration_targets` derived every non-t20 format's absolute-run gates by scaling t20's
empirically-chosen values by **`default_venue_score`** (od: 260/160 = 1.625 → MAE gate 40.6). Its
own comment had the right intent — *"a longer format naturally spreads over more raw runs"* — but
used the **mean** as the proxy for **spread**. MAE, bias and std-dev range are all *dispersion*
metrics; they scale with how widely innings totals spread, not with where they are centred, and
the two are not proportional.

Measured first-innings-total SD per format (full-length innings only, same population the
calibration script samples) versus the mean-based proxy actually in use:

| Format | Mean | SD | Old scale (mean-based) | New scale (SD-based) | MAE gate: old → new |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `t20` | 159.0 | 37.74 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 25.0 → 25.0 *(anchor, unchanged)* |
| `od` | 248.0 | 69.89 | 1.625 | 1.852 | 40.6 → **46.3** |
| `t20w` | 127.3 | 39.65 | 0.781 | 1.051 | 19.5 → **26.3** |
| `odw` | 225.0 | 66.24 | 1.381 | 1.755 | 34.5 → **43.9** |
| `hnd` | 146.0 | 27.36 | 0.875 | 0.725 | 21.9 → **18.1** |
| `hndw` | 127.4 | 23.06 | 0.791 | 0.611 | 19.8 → **15.3** |

The error was **bidirectional and large**. `t20w` is the clearest illustration: it has a *lower*
mean than `t20` (127 vs 159) but a *higher* absolute spread (39.7 vs 37.7), so mean-scaling made
its gates ~35% too tight — precisely backwards. `hnd`/`hndw` were too loose by a similar margin.

> [!NOTE]
> This also loosens od's **bias** gate (13.0 → 14.8). It does **not** retroactively justify
> skipping Phase 1: od's original +18.17 bias fails 14.8 too, so the damping work was necessary
> either way. Phase 1's shipped +12.72 clears both the old and new bias gate.

**Shipped:** `innings_total_sd` added to all six `FORMAT_SPECS` entries; `_calibration_targets`
(and its deliberate duplicate in `tests/score_feedback_shrink_sweep.py`) now scale by it. The
`ou_line` still derives from `default_venue_score` — it's a market line, i.e. a genuine location
parameter, so the mean *is* the right basis there. `t20`'s scale is 1.0 by construction, so its
thresholds and `test_simulation_calibration.py`'s hardcoded copies of them are untouched.
Existing `mc_calibration_metrics*.json` files had their **threshold fields recomputed in place** —
every measured value is byte-identical, since thresholds are pure functions of format spec, not
simulation output, making this a correction rather than a re-lock.

### Finding 2 (NOT actioned — genuine modelling question) — where the real headroom is

**Bias is exhausted as a lever.** Inverting each format's (bias, MAE) pair for the underlying
error SD, then asking what MAE would be at *zero* bias:

| Format | MAE today | MAE if ALL remaining bias removed | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `t20` | 27.57 | 27.56 | −0.02 |
| `od` | 46.42 | 45.29 | −1.13 |

Neither clears its gate on bias work alone. **MAE is now purely a spread/skill problem.**

**Normalised against each format's own zero-skill floor** (the MAE of just predicting the median
every time — measured empirically, no distributional assumption; total-score skew is only ≈−0.2
and the empirical floor agrees with the Gaussian `0.798σ` approximation to within 3%):

| Format | Zero-skill floor | MAE today | Skill vs zero-skill | R² needed for gate | R² achieved | R² from venue+team Elo (in-sample) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `t20` | 29.19 | 27.57 | **5.6% better** | 31.1% | ~16% | 13.3% |
| `od` | 55.51 | 46.42 | **16.4% better** | 47.0% | ~34% | 13.2% |

od's simulator extracts roughly **3× more skill relative to its own baseline** than t20's does —
longer innings average out per-ball noise, so od's achievable R² is structurally higher, which is
the underlying reason mean-scaling failed. **t20, not od, is where the modelling opportunity
actually is**: it sits only 5.6% better than a median-guess. But closing to 31% R² means roughly
doubling explained variance, and simple pre-match features cap out near 13% — that needs *new
information* (conditions, toss, pitch reports), not tuning.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **od's MAE still marginally fails even the corrected gate: 46.42 vs 46.3 (+0.12).** Do not read
> Finding 1 as "od now passes". Two caveats make this a coin-flip rather than a verdict: (a) the
> calibration MAE is measured on 100 sample matches, carrying roughly **±3 runs** of sampling noise
> at od's scale — the fast-fidelity run of the same config gave 46.11, which *passes*; and (b) the
> scaling basis is itself a judgement call — scaling by the empirical zero-skill floor instead of
> SD (arguably more apt, since that floor *is* an MAE) gives od a 47.5 gate, which it clears. od is
> best described as **sitting exactly at its corrected gate**, not comfortably inside it.

**Left open deliberately:** whether t20's anchor of 25.0 is itself attainable. It was
"empirically chosen" and has never been validated against a skill ceiling; the numbers above
suggest it is optimistic, but re-anchoring it is a judgement call about what standard the
simulator *should* meet, not a measurement — and it would move every format's gate at once.
